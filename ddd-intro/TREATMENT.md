# The Drawing Comes First — treatment

A ~72 s introduction to Document-Driven Development (github.com/s87343472/document-driven-development),
Blueprint style. 1920×1080, 24 fps. Two versions from one picture: English (Kokoro `af_heart`) and
Chinese (edge-tts `zh-CN-XiaoxiaoNeural`); every word on the sheet is localised, captions burned in, `.srt` for each.

## 1. Three candidate structures

A. **Two sheets, one table.** Left: a bridge built without drawings; right: the same bridge with the
method. Clear, but a split screen for 70 s halves every figure and leaves no room for five steps.

B. **A drawing set, sheet by sheet.** intent, spec, plan as three separate sheets laid on the table in
turn. Faithful to the documents, but it makes the film about paperwork, which the method itself says
to keep short; the checks and the business core get lost.

C. **One sheet, one bridge, five figures.** A bridge between two banks: the person who knows the
business on one side, the agent who knows how to build on the other, the documents as the deck where
they meet (the skill's own line). The hook is the bridge built from both banks with no drawing: the
halves miss in the middle. Then the sheet is drawn properly, FIG. 1 to FIG. 5, one step of the method
per figure, and the title block carries the verdict.

**Chosen: C.** The bridge is not decoration: it is the method's central sentence made literal, and a
failed bridge is the 3-hour debug bill everyone recognises. The opening (a hurried build that misses)
and the ending (the same span, drawn first, meeting and stamped) are the same place on the sheet.

## 2. Logline and arc

Logline: a bridge built from both banks without a drawing misses in the middle; drawn first, figure by
figure, it meets, holds, and is approved.

- **Setup** (0–15 s): hurried half-spans from each bank; a short dimension "15 MIN SAVED", then a long
  one running across the gap, "3 H DEBUG". The halves are erased. The sheet header is lettered.
- **Turn** (15–55 s): FIG. 1 business core → FIG. 2 what already exists → FIG. 3 short documents →
  FIG. 4 checks prove it (and a bug becomes a check) → FIG. 5 process matches the stakes.
- **Ending** (55–72 s): pull out to the whole sheet: person, documents, agent; silence; the red stamp
  lands in the title block; the install line is lettered in.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Patent drawings of the 19th century (Fig. 1 / Fig. 2 plates).** Learn: numbered figures as chapters,
  part balloons, a notes column that tells the story. Don't take: any real patent or machine.
- **Charles and Ray Eames, "Powers of Ten".** Learn: one continuous space where scale changes carry the
  argument; the final pull-out that shows everything at once. Don't take: the zoom route, the imagery.
- **Bach two-part inventions.** Learn: two interlocking voices as "person and agent" in the score. Don't
  take: any melody.

## 6. Cue map

96 bpm, 4/4. Harpsichord two-part invention (person and agent as two voices), bassoon bass, pizzicato
strings for the drafting, celesta for the checks.

| Bars | Section | Music |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Hook | one harpsichord voice, hurried and off-beat; it stops dead when the halves miss (5.0) |
| 5–6 | Title | the second voice enters in imitation: the invention begins |

## 8. Type and captions

- English: Architects Daughter (lettering, notes, captions), B612 (title-block fields), Allura (author
  signature). Chinese: Zhuque Fangsong (朱雀仿宋), the long-fangsong face Chinese engineering drawings use.
  All OFL.
- Captions are drawing notes in a translucent box with double rules, "NOTE / n", lettered on, placed per
  shot in empty sky; every line is also lettered into GENERAL NOTES.
- One non-blue colour, once: the red stamp.
